# hello PAL

A campaign site where PAL owners mint their PAL ID and turn it into epic stickers.

## Overview

**On-Chain Content (OCC)** is Gall3ry's framework for turning NFTs from static images in a wallet into content people can use, show off and share. **hello PAL** applied it to a time-limited campaign with the **XPLA** blockchain: owners of PAL characters customize their PAL into an ID card, use it as a sticker, and earn rewards by sharing it.

I designed the **campaign site** where users mint their PAL ID the way they want it and unlock their PAL epic stickers.

**Role:** Visual / Campaign Site Designer
**Client:** Gall3ry, in partnership with XPLA
**Scope:** Campaign site visual design, PAL ID card and sticker visuals, key mint screens
**Worked with:** Developers and Product Manager

---

## Approach

I worked with **developers** and a **product manager**, so the visuals were designed to ship, not just to look good in a file.

- **With the PM** — aligned on the goal of the campaign, what the user should do, and how the experience should feel before opening Figma.
- **With developers** — agreed early on how the PAL ID card, stickers and screens would be built, so the design stayed faithful once it went live.
- **Visual first** — the PAL character and its customization led the look of the site. The mint experience then followed it.

---

## Research

### Context

Gall3ry describes OCC as content that combines on-chain and off-chain data and lives as an NFT. The goal is to keep a community engaged after launch, not only at the moment of minting.

hello PAL is a good example of that idea. In the public announcement of the event, the partnership aimed to give NFT ownership a practical use by turning PAL characters into a way to communicate: ID cards used as messaging stickers, with rewards for sharing them.

### Flow architecture

Before designing screens, I mapped how the whole campaign fits together: from connecting a wallet and minting the PAL ID Card, through sticker creation and sync to Telegram, to sharing, random rewards, referral and leaderboards.

<!-- demo:hello-pal-flow -->

| Moment in the flow | What the user sees |
| --- | --- |
| Mint | PAL ID Card with the PAL NFT and wearables |
| Create | Sticker recommendation, creation and update |
| Share | OCC landing page with the owner's PAL sticker list |
| Reward | Random reward and reward claim |
| Telegram | PAL on-chain sticker, referral and value pack page |
| Compete | Leaderboard 1 and Leaderboard 2 |

## Reflection

hello PAL taught me a lot about designing experiences in Web3, especially for content that has been tokenized.

When content becomes a token, it is no longer just something to look at. It is something people own, show off and share. Here, a character became an ID card and then a sticker, so the design had to work as an object to own and as a way to talk to friends.

It also gave me a closer view of **meme NFT culture**: how communities express themselves through playful, recognizable visuals, and how much people care about making something feel like theirs. That pushed me to treat personality, humor and community identity as real design inputs, and to balance a fun, expressive look with a clear, simple mint experience.

hello PAL also became the starting point for my continued work with Gall3ry on more projects, such as StepN and Marblex.
